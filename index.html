01AN ACCOUNT OF SURVIVAL & REPAIR
01THE BOOK

The story beneath the story

Understanding the fire
without excusing the damage.

In this candid memoir, Daniel “Bret” Lingar traces a life shaped by premature birth, childhood violence, grief, addiction, and the fracture of family relationships.

The book moves from the remembered events into the narrator’s attempt to understand the patterns that followed: the alarm that never seemed to switch off, the relief that turned into another trap, and the shame that made repair harder. It does not promise a neat recovery or ask readers to confuse explanation with excuse.

The memoir holds two truths together: survival responses can make sense, and the harm they cause still matters.

02INSIDE THE STORY

Three threads, one life

What the book
tries to hold.

Not a diagnosis of anyone else. Not a clean redemption arc. A first-person account of what happened, what it cost, and what the narrator is still learning to do differently.

I

The body remembers

From an incubator and the blanket pulled over a frightened child to the adult who freezes under pressure, the memoir asks how early fear can shape a life—while acknowledging what the narrator cannot know for certain.

↘
II

Relief has a cost

Drugs, work, isolation, and relationships become ways to quiet pain. The story names the need beneath those choices without denying the damage they caused.

↘
III

Repair is lived, not promised

Estrangement, missed years, and difficult attempts at connection bring the memoir back to accountability: trust has to be built with behavior, and other people get to tell their side.

A note on the voice behind the book

Daniel “Bret”
Lingar

Daniel Lingar is an advocate, writer, and teacher focused on complex trauma, addiction recovery, and nervous-system regulation.

From the Storm to the Fire is his attempt to map the patterns without turning them into an excuse. The story stays unfinished by design: the work of recovery and repair is still happening.
